# M11 Change Control, Baseline and Decision Log

Status in the app: not built yet. This page describes the module as the BRD defines it, then what the development build shows today.

## What it is for

Change control answers the seventh core management question of the BRD: which commitments have changed, why, and who decided ([section 4.3](../requirements/Enterprise_Portfolio_Intelligence_Governance_Platform_BRD_v4.5.md#43-core-management-questions)). [Module M11 of BRD v4.5](../requirements/Enterprise_Portfolio_Intelligence_Governance_Platform_BRD_v4.5.md#m11-change-control-baseline--decision-log-chg) joins the change register of the earlier proof of concept (POC), which advises and shows the impact of a change, to baseline governance. Before a project is committed its plan is freely editable and the register only advises. After commitment the values locked at commitment change only through an approved change request (CR) (BR-04, BR-05), which is what the BRD calls baseline integrity ([section 4.4](../requirements/Enterprise_Portfolio_Intelligence_Governance_Platform_BRD_v4.5.md#44-product-principles)).

The register lists the CRs of a project, with counters and status filters, and says whether the project is baselined, in which case the register is enforced, or not, in which case it only advises (CHG-01). A CR records who asks, its type (scope, schedule, budget, resource, dependency or other) and priority, what is changing and why, the WBS items it affects, and its impact on schedule, budget and resources (CHG-02). The system lists the items the CR affects directly and the downstream items that could shift through dependency links. That analysis is advice: nothing is rescheduled (CHG-03, BR-23), in line with the principle "warn, do not auto-reschedule" of the BRD.

An approver decides with Approve, Reject, Defer or Request information, and a decision without a rationale is blocked (CHG-04, BR-30). The history of a CR, with actor and time, cannot be edited (CHG-05). The requester cannot be the only approver of the same CR unless the workspace policy allows self-approval for operational changes (CHG-09, BR-28). Each role sees only its own actions: the requester edits or withdraws, the approver decides and the project manager applies. Only a draft CR can be deleted; a submitted one is withdrawn or closed with a reason (CHG-12, BR-29).

On commitment, which is the charter approval or the gate the workspace configures, the system captures a baseline of scope (deliverables), dates, budget and critical dependencies, and locked values cannot be edited directly: editing a locked target date prompts the user to raise a CR (CHG-06, BR-04). An approved CR unlocks only the baseline fields it affects and shows "Approved pending application" until the project manager applies it, after seeing the difference between the current baseline and the new values; applying creates a new baseline version that refers to the CR. An approved CR left unapplied beyond the workspace threshold raises an attention item (CHG-07, BR-05, D-08).

The decision log stores the decisions on CRs, gates and the portfolio with their rationale, evidence links, approver and date. A decision cannot change after approval except through a correction workflow, and a journal entry of type Decision can be promoted into the log, which then links back to the entry (CHG-11; the journal belongs to [M12 Journal and Project Reporting](M12-journal-and-project-reporting.md)). A client connected through MCP can only prepare a decision, which a person then confirms in the application (MCP-08, BR-35).

The module is for project managers, who raise and apply CRs, for approvers and executives, who decide them (an executive decides major CRs, and the PMO the portfolio-level ones within its authority), and for anyone who later asks why a commitment moved. Ten of the twelve requirements are priority P0, part of Release 1. CHG-08 (a CR carries structured field changes that apply automatically on approval) and CHG-10 (thresholds on schedule, budget and FTE impact route a CR to the project manager, the PMO or an executive) are P1, which the BRD reads as V2. The BRD builds the module in the project execution layer for milestone 1A and expects, at milestone 1B, that gate and CR decisions flow into the decision log ([section 18.2](../requirements/Enterprise_Portfolio_Intelligence_Governance_Platform_BRD_v4.5.md#182-release-1-delivery-structure-d-01)). Its wireframes are screens 32 (Change requests and baseline), 12 (Gate review), 33 (Journal and project reporting) and 38 (My approvals). It takes part in the journeys Change request, Gate review, PM weekly update (step 5) and CEO weekly review (step 7) ([section 7.9](../requirements/Enterprise_Portfolio_Intelligence_Governance_Platform_BRD_v4.5.md#79-key-user-journeys)).

The onboarding addendum amends two requirements of the module ([addendum, section 13](../requirements/Enterprise_Portfolio_Intelligence_Governance_Platform_BRD_Addendum_Onboarding_v1.1.md#13-amendments-to-brd-v45)). A baseline captured at launch from imported values, for a project adopted as committed, is labelled "from import" and, like any baseline, changes only through CRs (CHG-06, BR-47). And the addendum assumes that CHG-10 is P0, because the executive's decision routing in onboarding uses its thresholds; BRD v4.5 itself still gives it P1, and the addendum's decision register still lists D-37 as a recommendation needed by Discovery, while the third external review counts it as taken (see Open questions).

## What users can do today

Nothing yet. The app is not deployed and has no back end. Its development build runs on mock data and holds one screen of the BRD, the project overview (screen 24, module M10), so no requirement of this module can be used: nobody can log, assess, decide or apply a CR, capture a baseline or read a decision log.

The build's navigation does name three of this module's screens. Each link opens the shared page for a screen that is not built, and all three screens are phase MVP in the wireframe manifest:

- Raise change request, the button in the project header of screen 24, links to `/w/[workspaceId]/projects/[projectId]/changes/new`, the route of screen 32.
- Governance, a tab of the same header, links to `/w/[workspaceId]/projects/[projectId]/governance`, the route of screen 12.
- Approvals, in the Home group of the sidebar, links to `/me/approvals`, the route of screen 38. At narrower widths the same link sits in the icon rail of a tablet, or in the drawer that the menu button opens.

The shared page shows, in the English interface, the title of the link (for example Raise change request), the sentence "Not built in the first batch.", the screen number, the path, and the filters that came in the address, or "None". It follows the rule of the record [Final routes, drill filters in the URL, and unbuilt destinations](../../architecture/decisions/routes-and-unbuilt-destinations.md): an MVP screen that is not built already has its final route and a generic page. The route table has no destination for screen 33 (Journal and project reporting, where a Decision entry is promoted to the log); see [M12 Journal and Project Reporting](M12-journal-and-project-reporting.md).

The only baseline data in the build are labels that the mock server sends to screen 24. The project header shows a tag with the baseline version and date, or "No baseline", and the Delayed tile shows "Not baselined" in place of a number when the server reports that the project has no baseline, as BRD section 12.1 asks for a project that has none yet. Of the two mock projects that have data, one has a baseline and one has none. The build captures and computes no baseline itself: the labels are values of the mock data (see [Screens call the real API; a mock server answers in mock builds only](../../architecture/decisions/mock-server-behind-real-api.md)).

## Open questions

- Does the decision log keep the AI-drafted rationale and the preparing client beside the person who confirmed? CHG-11 stores a decision with its rationale, evidence links, approver and date, and the wireframes mark a draft prepared through MCP as not decided until a person confirms it (screens 12 and 38); neither says what the log keeps once the person has confirmed. The first review recommended that the log record both the preparing client and the human confirmer, with the label "AI-drafted, edited by" when the AI had filled in the rationale (lines 750 to 755 of the original), and asked as its 30th question whether the log must keep the original AI draft or only the final text a person approved (line 898 of the original). MCP-11 logs a summary of the parameters of every MCP call, and the BRD's open question Q-12 asks whether the audit log keeps summaries only or full parameters and results. Source: [Pre-feasibility review of the BRD, round 1](../../history/reviews/metis-review-tien-kha-thi.md).
- Does CHG-10, the approval authority that routes a CR by its schedule, budget and FTE impact, move from P1 to P0? BRD v4.5 gives it P1 (V2). The onboarding addendum recommends P0 (decision D-37) because the executive's decision routing in onboarding uses these thresholds, and needs the decision by Discovery. The third external review lists D-37 with D-32 as already taken ([Round 3 review](../../history/reviews/metis-review-vong-3.md), line 841 of the original), while the addendum's register still names Discovery as the time it is needed. Source: the decision row in line 108 and the amendment row in line 16 of [Onboarding addendum, changes](../requirements/source/addendum-onboarding/parts/04-changes.md).
