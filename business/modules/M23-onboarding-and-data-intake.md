# M23 Onboarding and Data Intake

Status in the app: not built yet. This page describes the module as the BRD addendum defines it, then what the development build shows today.

## What it is for

Onboarding and Data Intake is the module that the onboarding addendum adds to the BRD. The main BRD v4.5 has no M23, and the addendum supplements it without editing it ([module M23 of addendum v1.1](../requirements/Enterprise_Portfolio_Intelligence_Governance_Platform_BRD_Addendum_Onboarding_v1.1.md#m23-onboarding--data-intake-onb)). Its goal is to take a new client from nothing to a working workspace in 30 minutes of the main setup person's active effort, without slide tours and without a long wizard. Setting up a tenant, a workspace with its lifecycle and approvals, its people and roles, and its running projects by hand takes dozens of steps, and the buyers are executives who will not fill in forms, so the module turns setup from filling in into confirming what Metis has read. The 30 minutes are the active open time of the main setup person, counted for sessions that meet stated entry conditions. Success is one event, activation, whose four parts must all hold in the same session: the workspace is Active with the company's own lifecycle published, at least one real person is invited, at least one real running project is adopted, and the executive cockpit shows at least one metric computed from the client's own data ([section 4.1 of the addendum](../requirements/Enterprise_Portfolio_Intelligence_Governance_Platform_BRD_Addendum_Onboarding_v1.1.md#41-goal-and-success-measure)).

The journey has six steps ([section 6 of the addendum](../requirements/Enterprise_Portfolio_Intelligence_Governance_Platform_BRD_Addendum_Onboarding_v1.1.md#6-the-onboarding-journey)): frame, drop, ask while reading, draft, approve and launch, and delegate the rest. When the provider operator creates the tenant, it records the scope, the main kind of work and the settings that cannot change later, taken from the sales discovery, and the executive confirms them in one tap together with three framing questions about their own view and decisions (ONB-02, ONB-03). The executive then drops whatever the company already has, such as trackers, decks, handbooks, plans and org charts. Metis reads it at once, with exact parsers first and AI only for what a parser cannot read (ONB-04, ONB-05), and shows a first signal from the client's own data as soon as one source is read (ONB-13). Everything it builds goes into a Draft workspace that only owners and admins can open (ONB-09), where running projects are adopted at their current stage (ONB-10). A gap engine turns what is missing into question cards for the executive, capped at 10 by default, and assumes, defaults, delegates or defers the rest (ONB-11, ONB-12). The executive sees their own cockpit as a draft and a rehearsal of the drafted approval flow on three real projects (ONB-14), then launches with one approval on a summary that leads with the outcomes reached, lists every role the launch would grant with the access it adds, and names what an undo cannot remove (ONB-15). The onboarding can be undone for a window after launch that is set per tenant (ONB-16). Owners named in the documents then receive pre-filled tasks, and launch never waits for them (ONB-17, ONB-19).

Metis builds and trains no model. When a tenant enables it, a third-party LLM called through an API reads the unstructured inputs and runs the questions, a choice the owner took on 2 Oct 2026 (D-32). That AI is one more client of Metis's own MCP server (module M05): it writes only through a prepare-only setup tool group into the Draft workspace and its onboarding session (ONB-06, ONB-07), and after launch it can only leave suggestions that a person accepts (ONB-18). Every value it creates keeps its source, a confidence band and a risk class, and a high-impact value is never assumed (ONB-08, BR-52); the addendum proposes identity and access, baseline budget and dates, approvers, quorum and routing, and settings that cannot change later as the high-impact fields (D-42, to be confirmed in Discovery). No customer content reaches the LLM provider before a tenant admin authorizes AI processing (ONB-22). A tenant that keeps the AI off still has two ways in: rule-based importers for structured files (ONB-20), and its own AI client, which uses the same tools while launch stays in Metis (ONB-21). The boundary around other tenants, Metis internals and person-only decisions is enforced on the server (ONB-23, ONB-24) and proven by three suites that run on every release and on every change of model, provider or prompt (ONB-25).

The requirements are split by release. ONB-01 to ONB-27 are priority P0, which belongs to Release 1 ([section 5.1 of the addendum](../requirements/Enterprise_Portfolio_Intelligence_Governance_Platform_BRD_Addendum_Onboarding_v1.1.md#51-in-scope-for-release-1)). They include the metrics of a session, with five clocks recorded separately (ONB-26), and the starting packs, one per kind of work offered in the pre-fill, which give each new workspace a lifecycle, option lists, an approval flow and references to built-in process frameworks (ONB-27). ONB-28 to ONB-40 are priority P1 and follow: voice notes and answers, a forwarding address, a one-time read of a cloud folder and of the company directory, reproduction of the last board pack, a walkthrough built from the client's own portfolio, pre-boarding from the sales cycle, an IT setup link, import into an Active workspace, a setup party with overnight completion, a human concierge mode, MCP prompts with a preview in the client's AI, and configuration suggestions from usage. The module has twelve wireframes, screens 46 to 57, all of them phase MVP in the wireframe manifest. The delivery plan of the addendum ([section 16](../requirements/Enterprise_Portfolio_Intelligence_Governance_Platform_BRD_Addendum_Onboarding_v1.1.md#16-delivery)) puts the session, intake, the agent, the setup tool group, the Draft workspace, adoption, the cards, the launch, the rule-based path, the authorization, boundary hardening and the three suites at milestone 1A, and the draft cockpit, the Portfolio X-ray as attention items, full delegation, the bring-your-own-AI path, the metrics and the dry run at milestone 1B.

The addendum is still a draft. The product owner took the decisions behind it, and approved its design section by section, on 2 Oct 2026. Among the numbered decisions of its section 17.1 only D-32 is marked as taken; the others (D-33 to D-39 and D-41 to D-43) are recommendations that the addendum assumes until they are confirmed, mostly in Discovery. Its status line reads "Draft for business and technology validation and for the external review", and its onboarding targets are proposals to confirm with the design partners in Discovery.

## What users can do today

Nothing yet. The app has no onboarding session, no file intake, no Draft workspace, no question cards and no launch, and no back end serves it: the development build runs on mock data ([Screens call the real API; a mock server answers in mock builds only](../../architecture/decisions/mock-server-behind-real-api.md)). None of the twelve onboarding screens, 46 to 57, has a route. The route table of the app (`src/core/routes.ts`) holds 22 destinations, and its table of screens names none of them. No tenant, workspace or project can be created in the app: the modules that would create them are not built either (M02 Tenant and Subscription Management, M03 Workspace Management and Configuration, M06 Project Charter and Business Case).

## Where the app differs from the BRD

None yet. Nothing of this module is built, so there is no built behavior to compare with the BRD addendum.

## Open questions

The first two come from the third external review, the next six are the addendum's own, and the last is a deferral that the addendum makes to the API design.

- A usability pass mark. A usability test with executives runs before the pilot; what share of them must complete the core path without help for the test to pass? The third external review proposes at least 80% and calls it a candidate threshold for Discovery to confirm, not a commitment; the proposed pass criteria of the addendum name no such share. Sources: [Round 3 review](../../history/reviews/metis-review-vong-3.md), line 270 of the original, and [part 4 of the addendum source](../requirements/source/addendum-onboarding/parts/04-changes.md), line 89.
- A changed framing answer. When an executive changes a framing answer after Metis has drafted a proposal, does Metis re-evaluate the proposal without creating duplicate records? The third external review proposes it as the interaction of the framing screen, and ONB-03 does not say what a later change does. Sources: [Round 3 review](../../history/reviews/metis-review-vong-3.md), line 351 of the original, and [part 2 of the addendum source](../requirements/source/addendum-onboarding/parts/02-requirements.md), line 13.
- Q-14. Which document types do the design partners actually hold? The answer orders the parser work (ONB-05) and fixes the reference sets of the accuracy suite.
- Q-15. Which languages must the onboarding agent handle in Release 1? It bears on the accuracy suite, the prompt pack and the cards.
- Q-16. Does EU AI Act Article 50 apply to Metis, and for which customers? Counsel is to confirm; it bears on the transparency duties of section 11.5 of the addendum.
- Q-17. Do the design partners' IT policies allow external MCP connections? It bears on the viability of the bring-your-own-AI path.
- Q-18. What AI spend per onboarding can the commercial plan bear? The answer sets the quotas and spend caps of ONB-24.
- Q-19. On the bring-your-own-AI path, does a quoted passage reported by the customer's AI count as evidence on its own, or must a person confirm it against the document before it can commit a project or lock a baseline? Until it is answered, the addendum assumes that a person confirms high-impact values on that path (BR-52, BR-53).
- The tool names, scopes and token lifetime of the setup tool group. The names in the addendum's catalogue are indicative, and the names, the scopes and the lifetime of the agent's token are to be fixed in the API design, not in the BRD.

Sources for Q-14 to Q-19: [section 17.2 of the addendum](../requirements/Enterprise_Portfolio_Intelligence_Governance_Platform_BRD_Addendum_Onboarding_v1.1.md#172-open-questions), lines 119 to 124 of [part 4 of the addendum source](../requirements/source/addendum-onboarding/parts/04-changes.md), and for Q-19 line 271 of [part 3](../requirements/source/addendum-onboarding/parts/03-design.md). Sources for the last item: line 54 of [part 2 of the addendum source](../requirements/source/addendum-onboarding/parts/02-requirements.md) and [Onboarding addendum: rulings made during execution](../../history/brd/addendum-onboarding/2026-10-03-execution-rulings.md), line 65 of the original.
